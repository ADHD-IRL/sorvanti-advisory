Using AI at work

Approved tools: your company AI assistant and AI features in Microsoft 365.

Go ahead

  • Draft internal emails, notes, and documents
  • Summarize public reports and articles
  • Brainstorm options, outlines, and questions

Check first

  • Anything a customer will see: a person reviews it before it goes out
  • Analysis that informs a decision
  • Trying a new AI tool or feature

Don't

  • Put client or personal data into unapproved tools
  • Let AI make final decisions about people or money
  • Present AI output as checked when it isn't

Not sure? Ask your manager or the AI lead before you go ahead.

What we answer

Five questions every leadership team should be able to answer

"How do we apply AI?" is really five questions. Each engagement answers them for your business, in writing, with the reasoning shown.

  1. Where does AI belong in our business, and where doesn't it?

    We look at the work first: where your people spend effort, where decisions are made, and where judgment creates value. AI belongs where it widens what people can do without hollowing out the expertise you depend on.

    You getA map of AI opportunity across functions, including what to leave alone.

  2. What should we do first, and why?

    Every opportunity is scored on value, readiness, and risk, then sequenced so early work builds the data, skills, and confidence that later work needs.

    You getA ranked portfolio and a sequenced roadmap.

  3. Which decisions must stay human?

    Some decisions are not delegable: framing the problem, accepting risk, setting priorities, signing off. We name them explicitly so AI is designed around them, not over them.

    You getA decision-rights view for each priority area.

  4. What will it take?

    People, data, technology, money, and governance. We size each one honestly, including the organizational change that most AI plans leave out.

    You getAn investment case and capability plan.

  5. How will we know it's working?

    Adoption counts aren't outcomes. We define measures tied to the work itself: decision quality, cycle time, capacity returned to people, and risk.

    You getA measurement framework and review cadence.
